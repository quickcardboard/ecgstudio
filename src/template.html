    Help

    Getting started

    ECG Studio computes the ECG forward from a beating heart. Activation spreads through about 37,000 tissue cells, each with its own action potential, and the twelve leads are calculated from the electrical field those cells produce. Every wave on the paper comes from the simulated tissue.

    • Left: 3D electrophysiologically animated model of the heart.
    • Right: a standard 12-lead layout with up to three 10-s rhythm strips, written by a sweeping cursor.
    • Drag the divider to resize the two sides, or press Expand on either side.
    • Mobile (landscape only): the Heart | ECG switch at the top shows one pane at a time.
    • Simulation controls:
      • Speed slows the whole simulation down to 1/20 of real time.
      • Pause freezes the animation.
      • Reset returns to normal sinus rhythm at 72 bpm with no abnormalities.

    View controls

    • External ↔ Cut: click either word to jump, or drag the slider between them. A cut plane facing you slides from outside the heart to its middle.
    • Anterior, 4-chamber, Lateral, Apex turn the camera to the predetermined view.
    • Labels: toggles structural labels on and off.
    • Leads: toggles lead direction references on and off.
    • Legend: toggles the colour legend panel.

    Control tabs

    Clicking a tab toggles its panel. A click on the heart acts on what is drawn where it lands, whichever tab is open: the conduction system (shown in the cut view) or the sinus node, a coronary artery, or else the muscle.

    • Rhythm: adjust the heart rate and toggle persistent arrhythmias. Click on the heart to stimulate an ectopic beat.
    • Blocks: only useful during a cutaway view. Click on the conduction system to start a block.
    • Ischemia: click a coronary artery to narrow it there (partial flow: subendocardial ischemia, ST depression and T changes that resolve), or double-click to occlude it (STEMI, then infarction). Use the time slider to see progression of the injury.
    • Conditions: add conditions such as chamber enlargement, WPW or pericarditis from the searchable list; remove them from the active list.
    • Learn: a review of the key features of this ECG and common pitfalls.

    About

    • Anatomy: the average adult four-chamber heart of Rodero et al. (2021), built from CT scans and voxelized at 2 mm. The left ventricular free wall is thickened by about 6 mm and the heart is placed in the chest with its apex behind the 5th intercostal space.
    • Electrophysiology: an event-driven cellular automaton with fibre-dependent conduction, AV-node decrement, restitution, transmural and apicobasal repolarization gradients, and a conduction system (SA node, AV node (dual pathways optional), His bundle, bundle branches, two left fascicles, Purkinje networks).
    • ECG: limb leads blend the lead field of a computed insulated torso with Einthoven's equilateral triangle; chest leads blend the heart-vector projection with the near-field potential at each electrode.
    • Calibration: parameters were tuned against textbook values for intervals, axes, voltages, bundle and fascicular blocks, hypertrophy criteria and the course of infarction. Some features are simplified or slightly exaggerated for teaching; the model is an aid to understanding, not a substitute for clinical ECGs.
    • Not a medical device: ECG Studio is for education only and must not be used for diagnosis or treatment.

    Getting started

    • Introduction: ECG Studio computes the ECG from a beating 3D heart.
    • Panels: tap a tab at the bottom to open its panel.
    • Simulation speed: 1× cycles the speed from 1× down to 1/20×. the pause button pauses; press and hold it to reset to sinus rhythm at 72 bpm.
    • Gestures:
      • Swipe the ECG to change lead group, and tap 5 s to change paper speed.
      • Double-tap the ECG for the full 12-lead ECG.
      • Double-tap beside the heart to expand the model with one live lead; tap the lead name to change it.
      • Tap the interpretation bar to expand it.
      • Drag to rotate the heart and pinch to zoom.
    • Learn button: top right of the heart; explains the key features and pitfalls of the current ECG.

    Control panel

    • Rhythm:
      • Choose a rhythm from the menu, set the rate, and give adenosine or a shock.
      • On the heart: tap an atrium or ventricle for an ectopic beat; three quick taps start flutter (atria) or VT (ventricles), four start AF or VF.
    • Blocks: opens the four-chamber cut. Tap the conduction system to cycle through its blocks.
    • View: External ↔ Cut slider, the Anterior / 4-chamber / Lateral / Apex views, and the Labels, Leads and Legend switches.
    • Ischemia: opens the outside view. From any tab, tap a coronary artery to narrow it (partial flow: ST depression and T changes) or double-tap to occlude it; taps elsewhere on the heart still give ectopic beats. Each region (for example proximal, mid or distal LAD) holds one marker, and one tap on it removes it. Use the time scrubber on the heart to follow the injury from onset to one month.
    • Conditions: add or remove chronic and acute heart conditions.

    About

    • Limitations: normal axis about 0–15°; LBBB/RBBB axes not textbook (read them on QRS shape); LAFB looks like an old anterior MI; distal LCx STEMI at 1 h not visible; an acute marginal (RV branch) occlusion shows inferior changes rather than the right-ventricular pattern; an old apical infarct also shows inferior Q waves; after an infarct the T inversion in V3–V5 can be deeper and last longer than usual; hyperkalemia T waves are peaked but broader-based than the classic tent; VT, VF and torsades slow older phones.
    • This is meant as an educational aid. Not a medical device or diagnostic tool.
