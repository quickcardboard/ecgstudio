Using LiveHeart

LiveHeart computes the ECG forward from the heart. A cellular automaton spreads activation through about 37,000 tissue cells. Each cell carries its own action potential. The 12 leads are calculated from the electrical field those cells produce, so every wave you see comes from the simulated tissue.

Screen

  • Left: the heart. Pink tissue is resting, blue is depolarizing, green is repolarizing.
  • Right: a standard 12-lead layout (2.5 s per column) with a 10 s lead II rhythm strip, written by a sweeping cursor.
  • Drag the divider to resize the two sides, or press Expand on either side. Double-click the divider to return to 50/50.

Controls

  • Sinus rate sets the sinus node rate (30–180 bpm). The rhythm stays sinus at every rate. Escape rhythms depend on where conduction is blocked, not on rate: a junctional escape (about 48 bpm) takes over when the sinus node or AV node is blocked, and a ventricular escape (40 bpm) when the block is below the AV node. Blocks arrive in milestone 2.
  • Time scale slows the whole simulation, down to 1/50 of real time, so you can follow the wavefront. The ECG slows with it.
  • View: External lets you rotate the heart. Cutaway shows the standard anterior four-chamber cut with the conduction system: SA node, AV node, His bundle, bundle branches and fascicles. The Purkinje network runs in the model but is not drawn.
  • Overlays: Labels names the chambers and vessels, and in the cutaway the conduction system too. Lead vectors draws the limb-lead hexaxial reference (amber; dashed lines are each lead's negative half), the chest electrodes V1–V6 with their lead vectors (blue), and the live heart vector (white). When the heart vector points along a lead's arrow, that lead records a positive deflection.
  • Pause freezes time. Reset restarts from rest.

Banner

The rhythm label comes from the simulator's own state, for example which pacemaker fired. PR, QRS and QT are measured from tissue activation and repolarization times. QTc uses Bazett's formula. The axis is measured from the QRS areas in leads I and aVF.

Model

The anatomy is the average adult four-chamber heart of Rodero et al. (2021), built from CT scans and voxelized at 2 mm. The model includes fibre-dependent conduction, AV-node decremental conduction, restitution, and transmural and apicobasal repolarization gradients with electrotonic modulation. Precordial leads are computed from electrode positions on the chest. Limb leads use the Einthoven projection of the heart vector.

Anatomy: Rodero C, Strocchi M, et al. "Virtual cohort of extreme and average four-chamber heart meshes from statistical shape model." Zenodo, 2021, doi:10.5281/zenodo.4593739. Licensed CC BY 4.0. LiveHeart is an educational model, not a diagnostic device.
