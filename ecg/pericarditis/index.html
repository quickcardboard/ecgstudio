Acute pericarditis

Acute pericarditis causes widespread concave ST elevation in most leads, PR-segment depression, and ST depression with PR elevation in aVR, without the reciprocal changes or Q waves of a myocardial infarction.

What the simulator reads

Normal sinus rhythm

Rate 72 bpm · PR 136 ms · QRS 85 ms · QT/QTcF 372/395 ms · Axis 0° · normal axis

Diffuse ST elevation (I, II, aVF, V2, V3, V4) with ST depression in aVR and PR depression in II (-0.09 mV): acute pericarditis pattern

ECG criteria

  • Diffuse concave ('saddle-shaped') ST elevation across several coronary territories, often I, II, aVL, aVF and V2–V6
  • PR-segment depression, with PR elevation and ST depression in aVR (and sometimes V1)
  • No reciprocal ST depression other than aVR and V1, and no Q waves
  • Spodick's sign: a downsloping TP segment; sinus tachycardia is common
  • Evolves in stages: ST elevation, then flattening, T inversion, and normalisation

Why it looks like this

Inflammation of the pericardium injures the thin layer of epicardial myocardium all around the heart. The injury is not confined to one artery's territory, so the ST elevation is diffuse; atrial epicardial injury depresses the PR segment.

Differential diagnosis

  • STEMI (territorial ST elevation, reciprocal depression, convex ST, Q waves)
  • Benign early repolarisation (no PR depression; ST/T ratio in V6 under 0.25)
  • Myocarditis
