Left atrial enlargement

Left atrial enlargement makes the P wave longer: a broad, notched P wave of 120 ms or more in lead II (P mitrale) and a deep, wide terminal negative part of the P wave in V1.

What the simulator reads

Normal sinus rhythm

Rate 72 bpm · PR 162 ms · QRS 85 ms · QT/QTcF 373/396 ms · Axis +29° · normal axis

Left atrial enlargement (P mitrale): P 153 ms wide, notched in II (humps 56 ms apart)

ECG criteria

  • P wave 120 ms or longer in II
  • Notched ('M-shaped') P wave with peaks over 40 ms apart
  • Terminal negative part of the P wave in V1 at least 1 mm deep and 40 ms wide (one small square each)
  • Common with LVH, mitral valve disease and hypertension

Why it looks like this

The left atrium is activated after the right, so its larger mass and longer conduction path prolong and enlarge the second half of the P wave. Because the left atrium lies posteriorly, that late force points away from V1 and appears as a negative terminal deflection there.

Differential diagnosis

  • Interatrial conduction delay (Bachmann's bundle block) without enlargement
  • Right atrial enlargement (tall rather than wide P)
  • Ectopic atrial rhythm
